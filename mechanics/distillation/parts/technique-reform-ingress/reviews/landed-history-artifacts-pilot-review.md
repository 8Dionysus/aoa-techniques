## What The Fourteenth Pilot Proved

- `history/` can hold a compact artifact shelf without turning saved session
  evidence into memory doctrine.
- Six related leaves can sit together without becoming one lifecycle pipeline:
  first-save capture, transcript packaging, local index, witness trace, replay,
  and code-lineage each kept its own executable center.
- Moving the shelf did not erase frontmatter truth. The path now names the
  browseable history-artifact neighborhood, while `domain: history` and
  `kind: artifact` still name the canonical facets.
- The first `history` trunk shelf complements the proof-side summary corridor:
  proof shelves can publish and validate evidence, while history shelves
  preserve saved artifacts as reviewable objects.
- The shelf works for small-agent selection because each leaf is still one
  bounded move rather than a platform for transcripts, memory, retention,
  replay, indexing, and lineage all at once.

## Remaining Weaknesses

- `AOA-T-0026` remains a promoted first-save anchor inside a mostly
  post-capture shelf. That asymmetry is useful now, but later history shelves
  should watch whether first-save capture needs its own sibling shelf.
- `AOA-T-0053` can still look like recall infrastructure if local indexes are
  treated as source authority instead of derivative lookup.
- `AOA-T-0045`, `AOA-T-0066`, and `AOA-T-0067` can attract downstream memory,
  replay-product, or repo-analytics pressure if later consumers skip the
  boundary notes.
- The shelf proves one history trunk neighborhood, not the full `history`
  topology. Future history shelves still need direct reading before movement.
- `family`, `tree_path`, capability, substrate, execution-profile, and risk
  axes are still scout or projection layers, not bundle frontmatter truth.

Reason:

`recovery/antifragility-recovery` is the next clean candidate because the
recovery trunk already has one successful `diagnosis-repair` shelf, while the
remaining antifragility-family bundles are still scattered between
`system-recovery/` and `validation-patterns/`. The direct-read question is
specific enough to test a second recovery shelf: degraded continuation,
shared-substrate stop, stress-event closeout, and receipt-first failure
analysis may form one recovery neighborhood without importing incident
response doctrine, runtime ownership, validation-patterns erasure, proof
authority, or a generic resilience platform.

Why direct-read first:

The four bundles are related but authority-sensitive. `AOA-T-0097` owns
degraded continuation and later regrounding, `AOA-T-0099` owns one isolated
service stop on shared substrate, `AOA-T-0100` owns stress receipt and
reviewed closeout, and `AOA-T-0098` owns evidence-first failure analysis from
receipts. The next review must decide whether moving `AOA-T-0098` from
`validation-patterns/` into a recovery shelf clarifies selection or hides its
validation nature. It must also decide whether the shelf should remain
`antifragility-recovery` or split before migration.

Why not another proof, instruction, execution, continuity, or history shelf
first:

The previous waves have tested continuity, ingest, recovery, instruction,
knowledge-lift, proof, and history trunks. Returning to recovery now is useful
because there is already one landed recovery precedent, but the candidate shelf
has cross-domain pressure that can sharpen the tree rules before broader
remaining shelves move.

## What The Twenty-Fourth Pilot Proved

- `proof/` can hold owner-truth closeout atoms without becoming owner law,
  release policy, GitHub policy, public-share approval policy, workspace
  install doctrine, or closeout automation.
- The shelf improves browsing because ingress guard posture, finding closeout,
  remote-owner endcaps, generated publish validation, and mirror parity now
  sit beside proof-facing evidence and summary-integrity shelves rather than
  staying split across broad `agent-workflows` and `docs` homes.
- Path placement can preserve `domain: agent-workflows`, `domain: docs`,
  distinct `kind` values, promoted status, support files, and canonical
  readiness holds.
- The shelf gives small agents a compact route question: what owner evidence
  must be named before entry, mutation, closeout, publish, remote landing, or
  mirror trust can honestly proceed?
- Generated projection rows converged on current paths after migration without
  becoming `tree_path` frontmatter truth.

  PR is treated as wider platform or product proof.

  publish validation becomes full rollout governance.

- The five leaves can still collapse into a vague closeout workflow if ingress
  guard, audit closeout, remote owner endcap, publish validation, and mirror
  parity are not kept as separate one-use atoms.
- All five leaves remain promoted rather than canonical; their readiness notes
  still ask for stronger independent reuse evidence.
- Future relation work should improve cross-shelf browsing without adding
  `tree_path`, `family`, capability, substrate, execution-profile, or risk
  frontmatter.

is closed by `owner-truth-closeout`. It gathers automation-worthiness,
approval sensitivity, seed routing, promotion-target rejection, local pattern
adoption, skill-proposal handoff, retention, and obsolescence pressure around
one governance question: when should repeated practice become automation,
adoption, skill proposal, retention, or retirement, and what authority does
that verdict not claim?

Why split review first:

The projection already marks all nine leaves as `split-review-needed`. The
first five come from automation and quest-harvest pressure, while the last
four come from method-growth adoption and retention pressure. They may still
form one governance tail, but moving them as one shelf without direct reading
would blur automation fit, owner-layer promotion, local adoption, skill
handoff, retention, and obsolescence into one overlarge automation package.

Why not neighboring shelves first:

`tool-use/tool-gateway` is still a singleton hold. `automation-governance` is
larger and riskier, but it is the next place where the tree must decide
whether to split, migrate, or activate reserved expansion steps before the
final singleton and closeout tail.

## Verdict

Accept `governance/promotion-boundary` as the twenty-sixth bounded migration
pilot.

The shelf holds after direct reading because all three bundles protect the
same owner-target moment: one reusable unit is close enough to promotion or
handoff that the next surface must stay explicit before anyone authors,
accepts, or activates the destination. `AOA-T-0089` emits one bounded
promotion verdict for one repeated reviewed quest unit. `AOA-T-0090` sharpens
that verdict by naming the nearest plausible wrong target. `AOA-T-0102`
packages technique-side skill pressure as a proposal packet without making
that packet a skill.

The acceptance is narrow. It does not create skill acceptance, skill
activation, quest/playbook promotion doctrine, role contract law, proof
verdict authority, memory write, routing policy, Method-growth law, local
owner consent, runtime behavior, KAG promotion, ToS canon, or broad
orchestration governance. It does not canonically promote these bundles,
does not change their `domain`, `kind`, status, evidence, relations,
maturity, validation-strength metadata, or public-safety posture, and does
not move files from this review pack alone.

## Why This Shelf Holds

- The common object is promotion boundary, not automation readiness or local
  adoption lifecycle.
- The three leaves form a compact before-authorship sequence: decide whether a
  repeated unit deserves a promotion verdict, reject the nearest wrong target,
  and when the destination is skill-shaped, hand off a bounded proposal packet.
- The shelf belongs under `governance` because the browsing question is which
  owner target may honestly receive the unit and which adjacent target must
  stay rejected before action.
- The shelf remains usable by small agents: pick one final verdict review, one
  nearest-wrong-target guardrail, or one skill-proposal packet, then stop.
- `AOA-T-0102` is related but not identical to `AOA-T-0089` and
  `AOA-T-0090`: it handles the skill-owner handoff case after technique-side
  pressure exists, while the first two keep quest promotion verdicts honest.

## Governance Trunk Fit

`promotion-boundary` fits the governance trunk because these techniques keep
owner-target choice, adjacent-target rejection, and receiving-owner handoff
explicit before any downstream surface is authored. They should not stay
buried inside broad `agent-workflows` once the tree migration can preserve
frontmatter truth: all three can move under
`techniques/governance/promotion-boundary/` while keeping their current
`domain`, `kind`, and promoted status unchanged.
